**Topic:** Spread option pricing — options on price difference of two assets  
**Category:** `probability-math`  
**Difficulty:** `advanced`  
**Language:** `python`  

### Summary
Spread options are derivative contracts whose payoff depends on the difference between the prices of two underlying assets, such as crack spreads in energy or calendar spreads in agriculture. Because the exact distribution of a difference of two log-normal prices does not have a closed-form density, valuation typically relies on analytical approximations like Margrabe's formula or Kirk's approximation.

### Key Points
- The payoff for a call spread option is $\max(S_1 - S_2 - K, 0)$ or $\max(F_1 - F_2 - K, 0)$ where $K$ is the strike price.
- Margrabe's formula provides an exact closed-form solution for the special case where the strike price $K = 0$ (effectively an option to exchange one asset for another).
- Kirk's approximation extends Margrabe's formula to handle a non-zero strike price by treating one asset plus the strike as a composite asset.
- Monte Carlo simulation is frequently required when path dependency is introduced or when multi-asset correlation structures become complex.

### Code Example
*Pricing a crack spread option using Kirk's approximation formula*

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import norm

def kirk_spread_call(F1, F2, K, T, sigma1, sigma2, rho):
    # Kirk's approximation for spread option on futures: max(F1 - F2 - K, 0)
    F2_adjusted = F2 + K
    sigma_diff = np.sqrt(sigma1**2 + (sigma2 * F2 / F2_adjusted)**2 - 2 * rho * sigma1 * sigma2 * F2 / F2_adjusted)
    d1 = (np.log(F1 / F2_adjusted) + 0.5 * sigma_diff**2 * T) / (sigma_diff * np.sqrt(T))
    d2 = d1 - sigma_diff * np.sqrt(T)
    return np.exp(-0.0 * T) * (F1 * norm.cdf(d1) - F2_adjusted * norm.cdf(d2))
```

### Best Practices
- ✅ Use Margrabe's formula as a benchmark sanity check when setting the strike price to zero.
- ✅ Validate approximation outputs against a high-accuracy Monte Carlo pricing engine, especially for deep out-of-the-money or long-maturity spreads.
- ✅ Carefully calibrate the cross-asset correlation parameter $\rho$, as spread option values are highly sensitive to it.

### Interview Questions
**Q:** What is Margrabe's formula used for, and what are its key limitations?  
**A:** Margrabe's formula prices an option to exchange one asset for another (equivalent to a spread option with a zero strike). Its primary limitation is that it cannot directly handle non-zero strike prices without modifications like Kirk's approximation.  

**Q:** How does correlation between the two underlying assets affect the price of a spread call option?  
**A:** An increase in correlation typically decreases the variance of the spread (since $\text{Var}(A-B) = \sigma_A^2 + \sigma_B^2 - 2\rho\sigma_A\sigma_B$), which lowers the time value and thus reduces the price of the spread option.

**Category:** `trading-strategies`

### Summary
Correlation trading is a quantitative strategy that profits from discrepancies between the implied correlation of multi-asset derivatives (like index options or basket swaps) and the realized correlation of their underlying components. It matters because it allows traders to express a pure view on the co-movement of assets independent of their individual directional price trends.

### Key Points
- Implied correlation is extracted from the prices of index options versus single-stock options, reflecting market expectations of future co-movement.
- Realized correlation is calculated retrospectively using historical return time series of the component assets over a specific window.
- Dispersion trading is a primary implementation of correlation trading, selling index volatility and buying single-stock volatility to profit from high constituent divergence.
- Correlation risk is highly non-linear, making position sizing and tail-risk hedging crucial due to sudden correlation spikes during market crashes.

### Code Example
*Calculates the rolling realized correlation matrix and extracts the average pairwise correlation for a basket of assets.*

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

def calculate_average_realized_correlation(returns: pd.DataFrame, window: int = 30) -> pd.Series:
    rolling_corr = returns.rolling(window=window).corr()
    n = returns.shape[1]
    # Extract upper triangle excluding diagonal to get pairwise correlations
    idx = np.triu_indices(n, k=1)
    avg_corr = rolling_corr.groupby(level=0).apply(lambda df: df.values[idx].mean())
    return avg_corr
```

### Best Practices
- ✅ Use exponential weighting or GARCH-based models to capture the time-varying nature of asset co-movements.
- ✅ Implement robust tail-risk protection, as market crashes routinely drive asset correlations toward 1.0 simultaneously.
- ✅ Monitor liquidity constraints of individual basket constituents when executing multi-leg dispersion structures.

### Interview Questions
**Q:** What is the core structural trade in a dispersion strategy?  
**A:** Selling index options (implied high correlation expectation) and buying single-stock options (implied low correlation expectation) across the index components.  

**Q:** Why do equity correlations tend to spike toward 1.0 during market downturns?  
**A:** Systemic panic, forced liquidations, and macro-driven risk-off sentiment override idiosyncratic company fundamentals, causing all stocks to sell off together.
