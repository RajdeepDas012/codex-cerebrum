### Summary
Mean reversion trading with Bollinger Bands exploits the statistical tendency of prices to return to their moving average after reaching extreme volatility boundaries. It provides a mathematical edge in sideways or ranging markets by identifying overbought and oversold conditions relative to recent price action.

### Key Points
- Bollinger Bands consist of a middle simple moving average (usually 20-period) flanked by upper and lower bands set a specified number of standard deviations (typically 2) away.
- Prices touching or piercing the outer bands indicate statistical extremes, signaling potential reversal points back toward the mean.
- Band width contraction (the 'squeeze') often precedes significant breakouts, which signals a warning to pause mean-reversion strategies.
- Success relies heavily on filtering for sideways or non-trending market regimes using indicators like the Average Directional Index (ADX).

### Code Example
*Calculates Bollinger Bands and generates mean reversion entry signals.*

```python
import pandas as pd

def bollinger_mean_reversion(df, window=20, num_std=2):
    df['sma'] = df['close'].rolling(window=window).mean()
    df['std'] = df['close'].rolling(window=window).std()
    df['upper'] = df['sma'] + (df['std'] * num_std)
    df['lower'] = df['sma'] - (df['std'] * num_std)
    df['signal'] = 0
    df.loc[df['close'] < df['lower'], 'signal'] = 1  # Buy oversold
    df.loc[df['close'] > df['upper'], 'signal'] = -1 # Sell overbought
    return df
```

### Key Points

- Standard deviation bands (e.g., ±1, ±2 standard deviations) are plotted around VWAP to measure overextended price moves.
- Trades are triggered when the price stretches to the outer deviation bands and then pulls back or reverses toward the central VWAP line.

### Code Example
*Calculates intraday VWAP and standard deviation bands using pandas*

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

def calculate_vwap_bands(df, num_std=2):
    typical_price = (df['high'] + df['low'] + df['close']) / 3
    cumulative_tp_vol = (typical_price * df['volume']).cumsum()
    cumulative_vol = df['volume'].cumsum()
    vwap = cumulative_tp_vol / cumulative_vol
    
    variance = (df['volume'] * (typical_price - vwap) ** 2).cumsum() / cumulative_vol
    stdev = np.sqrt(variance)
    
    df['vwap'] = vwap
    df['upper_band'] = vwap + (num_std * stdev)
    df['lower_band'] = vwap - (num_std * stdev)
    return df
```

```python
def altman_z_score(wc, re, ebit, ta, mve, tl, sales):
```

### Summary

### Key Points
- Contango is the normal market state where longer-dated VIX futures trade at a premium to near-term futures, causing a persistent drag on short volatility ETFs like UVXY or VXX due to roll yield.
- Backwardation occurs during market stress or crashes when front-month VIX futures spike significantly above longer-dated contracts, indicating extreme short-term panic.
- The VIX futures roll process requires selling expiring contracts and buying next-month contracts, which loses money in contango and gains money in backwardation.
- The VIX/VIX3M ratio or the slope between the first and second month futures (M1/M2) are primary quantitative gauges used to time volatility entries and exits.

```python
    else:

```

Earnings season patterns describe predictable stock price behaviors surrounding quarterly reports, notably the 'pre-earnings run' where prices drift higher on anticipation and the 'post-earnings drop' where stocks pull back regardless of good news due to profit-taking or lowered forward guidance. Understanding this dynamic is crucial for timing trades and avoiding the volatility trap known as 'volatility crush'.

### Key Points
- The pre-earnings run-up is often driven by speculative buying, analyst upgrades, and positive sentiment momentum leading up to the report date.
- The post-earnings drop or sell-off frequently happens ('sell the news') even when a company beats consensus earnings and revenue estimates.
- Implied volatility typically spikes heading into the announcement and collapses immediately afterward, impacting options pricing significantly.
- Forward guidance and management commentary during the earnings call carry far more weight on post-earnings price direction than historical quarterly results.

### Code Example
*Calculate historical pre-earnings drift and post-earnings change for a ticker*

```python
import pandas as pd
def analyze_earnings_drift(df, event_date):
    run_up = df.loc[event_date - pd.Timedelta(days=14):event_date, 'Close'].pct_change().sum()
    post_drop = df.loc[event_date:event_date + pd.Timedelta(days=3), 'Close'].pct_change().sum()
    return run_up, post_drop
```

```python
def analyze_earnings_drift(df, event_date):
```

- ✅ Utilize defined-risk options strategies (like iron condors or credit spreads) if trading options through earnings to mitigate volatility risk.

### Interview Questions
**Q:** Why might a stock drop significantly immediately after reporting an earnings beat that exceeded analyst expectations?  
**A:** This is a classic 'sell the news' event where the good news was already priced in during the pre-earnings run-up, or the company's forward guidance for the next quarter disappointed investors.  

**Q:** What happens to option implied volatility immediately after a company releases its earnings report, and how does it affect option holders?  
**A:** Implied volatility experiences a sharp drop known as 'volatility crush'. This reduces the extrinsic value of options rapidly, causing both long calls and long puts to lose value even if the underlying stock moves in the anticipated direction.

**Topic:** Revenue beat vs earnings beat — which matters more for stock reaction  
**Category:** `stocks-markets`  
**Difficulty:** `intermediate`  
**Language:** `language-agnostic`  

### Summary
Comparing revenue beats (top-line growth) to earnings beats (bottom-line profitability) helps analysts determine the true health and future trajectory of a company. While an earnings beat can be manufactured through cost-cutting and accounting adjustments, a revenue beat demonstrates genuine market demand, making top-line surprises often more sustainable drivers of long-term stock appreciation.

### Key Points
- Revenue represents total sales (top-line), while earnings represent net profit after expenses (bottom-line).
- Earnings can be artificially inflated in the short term via aggressive cost reductions, buybacks, or tax strategies.
- A revenue beat proves actual customer demand and product adoption, which is harder to fake.
- Market reactions often depend on the company's lifecycle: high-growth firms are heavily penalized for revenue misses, while mature value firms are judged more on earnings and cash flow.

### Code Example
*Demonstrates calculating top-line vs bottom-line surprise percentages and classifying stock reaction potential*

```python
def evaluate_earnings_surprise(est_rev, act_rev, est_eps, act_eps):
    rev_surprise = (act_rev - est_rev) / est_rev
    eps_surprise = (act_eps - est_eps) / abs(est_eps)
    if rev_surprise > 0 and eps_surprise > 0:
        return "Strong Bullish: High quality beat"
    elif rev_surprise < 0 and eps_surprise > 0:
        return "Cautious: Cost-cutting driven beat"
    return "Mixed or Bearish"
print(evaluate_earnings_surprise(100, 105, 1.5, 1.6))
```

**❌ Common mistake:**
```python
def evaluate_earnings_surprise(est_rev, act_rev, est_eps, act_eps):
    # Mistake: assuming absolute EPS value without checking for negative base or ignoring revenue entirely
    if act_eps > est_eps:
        return "Always Bullish"
print(evaluate_earnings_surprise(100, 90, 1.5, 1.6))
```

### Best Practices
- ✅ Always analyze revenue growth alongside gross margin trends to ensure sales are not being bought at the expense of profitability.
- ✅ Check forward guidance, as management's outlook often outweighs the backward-looking quarterly beat or miss.
- ✅ Differentiate between operating income growth and net income inflation driven by one-time tax benefits.

### Interview Questions
**Q:** Why might a company beat earnings estimates but see its stock price drop significantly?  
**A:** The earnings beat may have been driven entirely by aggressive cost-cutting or one-time tax benefits rather than core business growth, while top-line revenue missed expectations.  

**Q:** In what type of companies is a revenue beat typically valued more than an earnings beat?  
**A:** High-growth companies (like early-stage tech or SaaS firms), where capturing market share and top-line scaling take precedence over immediate profitability.
