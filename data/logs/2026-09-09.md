### Summary
The Turtle Trading System is a famous, fully mechanical trend-following breakout strategy developed by Richard Dennis and William Eckhardt. It utilizes Donchian channels for entry and exit signals, combined with a revolutionary volatility-based position sizing metric called ATR (Average True Range) known as 'Unit' sizing.

### Key Points
- System 1 uses a 20-day breakout for entry and a 10-day breakout in the opposite direction for exit.
- System 2 uses a longer-term 55-day breakout for entry and a 20-day breakout in the opposite direction for exit.
- Position sizing is determined by the Average True Range (ATR), where one 'Unit' equals 1% of total account equity divided by the dollar ATR.
- Pyramiding adds new units to winning positions as price moves favorably in increments of 0.5 ATR up to a maximum unit cap.

### Code Example
*Calculates Turtle system entry signals and ATR-based position sizing*

```python
import numpy as np

def turtle_signal(prices, highs_20, lows_10):
    long_entry = prices[-1] > np.max(highs_20[-20:])
    short_exit = prices[-1] < np.min(lows_10[-10:])
    return long_entry, short_exit
```

**Category:** `trading-strategies`  
**Difficulty:** `intermediate`  
**Language:** `language-agnostic`  

### Summary
Dual momentum is a quantitative investment strategy pioneered by Gary Antonacci that combines relative strength momentum with absolute momentum (trend-following). It selects the strongest performing asset from a peer group only if it exhibits a positive absolute return, otherwise rotating safely into a cash or fixed-income alternative.

### Key Points
- Relative momentum ranks a universe of assets to identify the top performer over a specific lookback window (e.g., 6 or 12 months).
- Absolute momentum acts as a risk filter, requiring the chosen asset's total return over the lookback period to be greater than a risk-free rate or zero.
- If the top relative asset fails the absolute momentum test, the capital is reallocated to a defensive asset such as Treasury bills or short-term bonds.
- This dual-layer approach aims to capture major bull market trends while drastically reducing drawdowns during prolonged bear markets.

### Code Example
*Calculates relative ranking and absolute threshold filtering for dual momentum asset allocation.*

```python
def dual_momentum_selection(returns_df, risk_free_return, lookback):
    momentum = returns_df.iloc[-lookback:].sum()
    top_asset = momentum.idxmax()
    if momentum[top_asset] > risk_free_return:
        return top_asset
    return 'Cash_Safe_Asset'
```

### Best Practices
- ✅ Use a standard 12-month lookback window to balance trend responsiveness with stability.
- ✅ Incorporate tax-advantaged accounts or low-cost ETFs to minimize the drag of periodic portfolio rebalancing.
- ✅ Maintain strict monthly or quarterly rebalancing discipline without second-guessing system signals during high volatility.

### Interview Questions
**Q:** What is the primary difference between relative momentum and absolute momentum in a dual momentum framework?  
**A:** Relative momentum compares an asset's performance against a peer group to find the leader, while absolute momentum evaluates an asset's return against an absolute hurdle rate (like cash) to ensure it has a positive trend.  

**Q:** Why is the absolute momentum filter critical during prolonged bear markets?  
**A:** It prevents the portfolio from holding relative 'winners' that are still experiencing absolute negative returns, forcing a rotation into safe assets like cash or T-bills to limit drawdowns.

### Summary
Trend following with Average True Range (ATR) dynamically adjusts position sizes based on current market volatility. By sizing trades inversely to ATR, it equalizes the dollar risk across assets with wildly different price behaviors, protecting capital during high-volatility regimes.

### Key Points
- ATR measures absolute price volatility over a specified lookback period, typically 14 days.
- Position sizing formula equates risk: Shares = (Total Account Capital * Risk Percentage) / (ATR * Multiplier).
- High volatility instruments receive smaller position sizes to maintain a constant risk profile.
- Low volatility instruments receive larger position sizes to ensure meaningful capital exposure.

### Code Example
*Calculates the exact share quantity to trade based on a fixed percentage account risk and ATR stop loss distance.*

```python
def calculate_position_size(capital, risk_pct, atr, multiplier):
    dollar_risk = capital * risk_pct
    risk_per_share = atr * multiplier
    shares = dollar_risk / risk_per_share
    return int(shares)
```

**❌ Common mistake:**
```python
def calculate_position_size(capital, price, atr):
    return capital / (price + atr) # Incorrectly ignores account risk tolerance and uses absolute price instead of volatility risk
```

### Best Practices
- ✅ Recalculate ATR and adjust position sizes periodically, especially during structural shifts in market volatility.
- ✅ Combine ATR position sizing with a trailing stop based on multiple ATR values from the highest high.
- ✅ Cap maximum leverage even if low ATR suggests taking an exceptionally large position.

### Interview Questions
**Q:** How does ATR-based position sizing differ from a fixed capital allocation strategy?  
**A:** Fixed capital allocates the same dollar amount to every trade regardless of risk, whereas ATR sizing allocates varying capital amounts so that the dollar risk per trade remains constant relative to market volatility.  

**Q:** Why is using a multiplier (like 2x or 3x ATR) important for stop-loss placement in trend following?  
**A:** It provides a buffer against normal market noise, preventing premature stop-outs while still cutting losses if the underlying trend breaks.

**Topic:** Mean reversion with Bollinger Bands — statistical edge in ranging markets  
**Category:** `trading-strategies`  
**Difficulty:** `intermediate`  
**Language:** `language-agnostic`  

### Summary
Mean reversion trading with Bollinger Bands exploits the statistical tendency of prices to return to their moving average after reaching extreme volatility boundaries. It provides a mathematical edge in sideways or ranging markets by identifying overbought and oversold conditions relative to recent price action.

### Key Points
- Bollinger Bands consist of a middle simple moving average (usually 20-period) flanked by upper and lower bands set a specified number of standard deviations (typically 2) away.
- Prices touching or piercing the outer bands indicate statistical extremes, signaling potential reversal points back toward the mean.
- Band width contraction (the 'squeeze') often precedes significant breakouts, which signals a warning to pause mean-reversion strategies.
- Success relies heavily on filtering for sideways or non-trending market regimes using indicators like the Average Directional Index (ADX).

### Code Example
*Calculates Bollinger Bands and generates mean reversion entry signals.*

```python
import pandas as pd

def bollinger_mean_reversion(df, window=20, num_std=2):
    df['sma'] = df['close'].rolling(window=window).mean()
    df['std'] = df['close'].rolling(window=window).std()
    df['upper'] = df['sma'] + (df['std'] * num_std)
    df['lower'] = df['sma'] - (df['std'] * num_std)
    df['signal'] = 0
    df.loc[df['close'] < df['lower'], 'signal'] = 1  # Buy oversold
    df.loc[df['close'] > df['upper'], 'signal'] = -1 # Sell overbought
    return df
```

**❌ Common mistake:**

```python
import pandas as pd

    return df
```

### Best Practices
- ✅ Combine Bollinger Bands with a trend filter like ADX or a 200-period moving average to ensure the market is truly ranging.
- ✅ Use secondary oscillators like RSI or Stochastic to confirm overbought/oversold extremes before executing trades.
- ✅ Set strict stop-losses just beyond the outer bands or recent swing highs/lows.

### Interview Questions
**Q:** How do Bollinger Bands dynamically adjust to market volatility?  
**A:** They use standard deviation as their scaling factor, widening during periods of high volatility and contracting during low volatility.  

**Q:** What is 'walking the band' and why is it dangerous for mean-reversion traders?  
**A:** It occurs when strong momentum pushes the price to continuously ride along the outer band without reverting; mean-reversion traders taking counter-trend positions during this time will face severe drawdowns.

### Summary
The opening range breakout strategy involves tracking the high and low prices established during the first 30 minutes of the market session and entering trades when price breaks out of this range. It matters because it captures early institutional momentum and high-volatility moves right after the opening bell.

### Key Points
- Defines the initial trading range by marking the high and low of the first 30 minutes of the market open.
- Triggers a long entry when the price breaks above the opening 30-minute high with volume confirmation.
- Triggers a short entry when the price breaks below the opening 30-minute low.
- Typically utilizes the opposite side of the opening range or the midpoint as an initial stop-loss level.

### Code Example
*Calculates the 30-minute opening range and generates breakout signals*

```python
import pandas as pd
def opening_range_breakout(df):
    market_open = df.between_time('09:30', '10:00')
    or_high = market_open['High'].max()
    or_low = market_open['Low'].min()
    post_open = df.between_time('10:00', '16:00')
    signals = pd.Series(0, index=post_open.index)
    signals[post_open['Close'] > or_high] = 1
    signals[post_open['Close'] < or_low] = -1
    return signals
```

### Best Practices
- ✅ Wait for a complete 5-minute or 15-minute candle to close outside the 30-minute range before entering.
- ✅ Align the opening range breakout direction with the broader daily or weekly trend to improve win rates.
- ✅ Implement strict stop-loss orders just inside the opposite boundary of the opening range or at the VWAP.

### Interview Questions
**Q:** How do you filter out false breakouts during an opening range strategy?  
**A:** By requiring volume confirmation above the recent average and waiting for a candle close outside the range rather than a momentary tick.  

**Q:** Where is the standard stop-loss placed in an opening range breakout trade?  
**A:** Usually at the opposite end of the opening range or at the midpoint (50% level) of the opening range.
