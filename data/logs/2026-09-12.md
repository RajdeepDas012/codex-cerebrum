### Summary
Fund flow analysis tracks the aggregation of capital entering or leaving specific market sectors, asset classes, or index funds. It matters because sustained capital shifts reveal institutional positioning and anticipated macroeconomic trends before price action fully reflects them.

### Key Points
- Measures net institutional and retail capital allocation across distinct industry groups
- Helps identify structural sector rotation from defensive plays to cyclical growth
- Divergences between rising prices and declining inflows can warn of exhaustion
- Aggregated via ETF creation/redemption data and mutual fund flow reports

### Code Example
*Calculates the 5-day rolling net capital flow using ETF volume and price change proxy.*

```python
import pandas as pd
def calculate_sector_flow(df):
    df['Dollar_Volume'] = df['Close'] * df['Volume']
    df['Estimated_Flow'] = df['Dollar_Volume'] * df['Daily_Return'].apply(lambda x: 1 if x > 0 else -1)
    return df['Estimated_Flow'].rolling(window=5).sum()
```

## Run #600 — 00:04 UTC

**Topic:** Margin call cascade — forced selling creating liquidity crisis  
**Category:** `stocks-markets`  
**Difficulty:** `advanced`  
**Language:** `language-agnostic`  

### Summary
A margin call cascade occurs when declining asset prices trigger widespread collateral deficits, forcing brokers to liquidate client positions regardless of fundamental value. This creates a self-reinforcing downward price spiral that can rapidly deplete market liquidity and trigger systemic financial stress.

### Key Points
- Brokers issue margin calls when an investor's equity falls below the maintenance margin requirement due to price drops.
- If the investor fails to deposit additional cash or securities, the broker automatically liquidates assets at market prices.
- Forced liquidations increase selling pressure, driving prices lower and triggering subsequent margin calls for other leveraged accounts.
- The feedback loop spreads across asset classes as institutions deleverage to meet liquidity demands, seizing up credit markets.

### Code Example
*Simulates a multi-step margin call cascade across leveraged portfolios*

```python
def simulate_cascade(prices, leverage, threshold):
    portfolio_value = prices * leverage
    equity = portfolio_value - (prices * (leverage - 1))
    margin_ratio = equity / portfolio_value
    forced_sells = margin_ratio < threshold
    return forced_sells
```

**❌ Common mistake:**
```python
def simulate_cascade(prices, leverage, threshold):
    return prices * leverage < threshold
```

### Best Practices
- ✅ Maintain conservative leverage ratios well below broker-mandated maintenance requirements to withstand sharp drawdowns.
- ✅ Monitor cross-asset liquidity indicators and funding spreads to detect early systemic deleveraging phases.
- ✅ Diversify funding sources and maintain a cash buffer to prevent forced liquidation of core long-term holdings.

### Interview Questions
**Q:** What is the primary mechanism that turns isolated price declines into a market-wide liquidity crisis during a margin cascade?  
**A:** Forced liquidation by brokers selling assets at any available price to cover deficits, which depresses prices further and triggers the next tier of margin calls.  

**Q:** How do prime brokers mitigate the risk of cascading defaults among their institutional clients?  
**A:** By enforcing stricter margin requirements, conducting intraday risk monitoring, and demanding higher haircuts on volatile collateral.
