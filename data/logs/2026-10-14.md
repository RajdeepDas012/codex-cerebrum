### Summary
A lock-free concurrent Cuckoo Filter improves upon Bloom filters by allowing dynamic item deletions and offering higher space efficiency for approximate membership testing. By leveraging atomic bucket-relocating and finger-printing CAS primitives along with hazard pointers, multiple threads can safely insert, delete, and look up elements simultaneously without explicit locking.

### Key Points

- Employs hazard pointer memory reclamation to prevent premature freeing of buckets or nodes during concurrent displacement chains.
- Supports space-efficient item deletion natively, a major drawback of standard Bloom filters which suffer from false deletion issues.
- Ensures high-throughput multi-core approximate membership testing suitable for real-time network security packet filtering and concurrent caches.

### Interview Questions
**Q:** How do Cuckoo Filters handle deletions compared to standard Bloom filters?  
**A:** Cuckoo filters store compact fingerprints of items in specific alternative buckets, allowing exact matches to be removed by clearing the slot without affecting other items. Bloom filters use bit arrays where clearing a bit could disrupt shared hash representations of other elements.  

**Q:** What is the primary synchronization challenge when implementing a lock-free Cuckoo Filter?  
**A:** The multi-step displacement chain (cuckoo hashing) where inserting an item requires kicking out an existing item to an alternate bucket, which must be done atomically via CAS to prevent race conditions among concurrent writer threads.
