**Category:** `trading-strategies`  
**Difficulty:** `advanced`

### Summary
Vanna measures the sensitivity of option delta to changes in implied volatility, while charm measures the sensitivity of option delta to the passage of time (delta decay). Understanding these second-order Greeks allows advanced traders to anticipate subtle, non-linear shifts in market maker hedging flows before they become obvious in first-order Greeks.

### Key Points
- Vanna links spot price movements with changes in volatility, driving accelerated dealer re-hedging when IV spikes during market selloffs.
- Charm (or delta decay) dictates how option delta drifts systematically as expiration approaches, particularly forcing steady flows near OpEx.
- Positive vanna in out-of-the-money calls means rising volatility increases their delta, forcing dealers to buy underlying stock.
- Charm effects intensify drastically during the final week of options expiration, creating predictable intraday directional biases.

### Code Example
*Calculates approximate vanna and charm for a European option using Black-Scholes formulas.*

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import norm

def calculate_vanna_charm(S, K, T, r, sigma):
    d1 = (np.log(S / K) + (r + 0.5 * sigma**2) * T) / (sigma * np.sqrt(T))
    d2 = d1 - sigma * np.sqrt(T)
    vanna = -np.exp(-d1**2 / 2) * d2 / (sqrt_2_pi := np.sqrt(2 * np.pi) * sigma * S) * S # simplified
    charm = -np.exp(-d1**2 / 2) * (2 * r * T - d2 * sigma * np.sqrt(T)) / (2 * T * sigma * np.sqrt(2 * np.pi))
    return vanna, charm
```

### Summary
Implied Volatility Rank (IVR) measures where a stock's current implied volatility sits relative to its high and low extremes over a specific historical lookback period, typically one year. It matters because raw IV numbers cannot be compared across different stocks, whereas IVR normalizes the value on a 0 to 100 scale to help options traders identify expensive or cheap premium.

### Key Points
- Calculated as (Current IV - 52-week Low IV) / (52-week High IV - 52-week Low IV) * 100.
- Normalizes volatility so a high-priced stock with 40% IV can be accurately compared to a low-priced stock with 20% IV.
- An IVR of 100 means current implied volatility is at its highest point over the past year.
- An IVR of 0 means current implied volatility is at its lowest point over the past year.

### Code Example
*Calculates Implied Volatility Rank given current IV and a historical array of IV values*

```python
def calculate_ivr(current_iv, historical_ivs):
    iv_low = min(historical_ivs)
    iv_high = max(historical_ivs)
    if iv_high == iv_low:
        return 0.0
    return ((current_iv - iv_low) / (iv_high - iv_low)) * 100
```

```python
import pandas as pd

def calculate_ivp(iv_series, lookback=252):
    recent_iv = iv_series.iloc[-lookback:]
```

### Summary
A vertical spread involves buying and selling options of the same type and expiration but at different strikes, creating a direct trade-off between win rate and payout. ### Key Points
- Debit spreads (bull call or bear put) are net cash outflows, meaning maximum loss is strictly limited to the upfront premium paid.
- Credit spreads (bull put or bear call) are net cash inflows, meaning maximum profit is capped at the initial credit received.
- Credit spreads generally offer higher probabilities of profit (PoP) because they win if the underlying stays still, moves in favor, or moves slightly against the position.
- Risk-to-reward ratios are inverted: debit spreads risk a small amount to make a larger amount, while credit spreads risk a larger amount to collect a smaller upfront credit.

### Code Example
*Calculates max profit, max loss, and risk-reward ratio for both debit and credit vertical spreads.*

```python
def analyze_vertical_spread(long_strike, short_strike, net_price, is_debit):
    width = abs(long_strike - short_strike)
    if is_debit:
        max_loss = net_price
        max_profit = width - net_price
    else:
        max_credit = net_price
        max_loss = width - net_price
        max_profit = max_credit
    return max_profit, max_loss
```

**❌ Common mistake:**
```python
def analyze_vertical_spread(long_strike, short_strike, net_price, is_debit):
    # Mistake: treating credit spread risk as just the net price instead of width minus credit
    return width - net_price, net_price
```

### Best Practices
- ✅ Select debit spreads when you have strong directional conviction and expect an expansion in implied volatility.
- ✅ Deploy credit spreads in flat or neutral-to-mildly directional environments where you want time decay (theta) to work in your favor.
- ✅ Target a minimum risk-to-reward ratio of 1:1 for debit spreads and ensure credit spreads collect at least one-third of the total strike width.

### Interview Questions

**Q:** Why might a trader choose a credit spread over a debit spread despite having a less favorable risk-to-reward ratio?

### Code Example

```python
def calculate_straddle_metrics(strike, call_price, put_price):

    upper_breakeven = strike + total_premium
    lower_breakeven = strike - total_premium
```

### Code Example

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

def calculate_zscore(series1, series2, window=30):
    hedge_ratio = np.cov(series1, series2)[0, 1] / np.var(series2)
    spread = series1 - hedge_ratio * series2
    rolling_mean = spread.rolling(window=window).mean()
    rolling_std = spread.rolling(window=window).std()
    zscore = (spread - rolling_mean) / rolling_std
    return zscore
```

### Summary
The ETF arbitrage mechanism relies on Authorized Participants (APs) who interact directly with the ETF issuer to create or redeem shares in large blocks called creation units. ### Key Points

- Creation involves an AP buying the underlying basket of securities and delivering them to the issuer in exchange for a fresh block of ETF shares.
- Redemption works in reverse: the AP delivers ETF shares back to the issuer in exchange for the underlying basket of securities.
- Market price deviations (premiums or discounts) trigger AP arbitrage, pushing the ETF price back toward its intrinsic NAV.

### Code Example
*Simulates ETF creation/redemption arbitrage profit calculation based on NAV and market price divergence*

```python
def calculate_arbitrage_profit(etf_market_price, nav, shares_per_unit, transaction_cost):
    if etf_market_price > nav:
        # ETF is at a premium: Buy basket, create ETF shares, sell ETF on open market
        gross_profit = (etf_market_price - nav) * shares_per_unit
        net_profit = gross_profit - transaction_cost
        return "Create", net_profit
    elif etf_market_price < nav:
        # ETF is at a discount: Buy ETF on open market, redeem for basket, sell basket
        gross_profit = (nav - etf_market_price) * shares_per_unit
        net_profit = gross_profit - transaction_cost
        return "Redeem", net_profit
    return "None", 0.0
```

### Code Example
*Calculates theoretical equity volatility implied by CDS spreads using a simplified Merton model framework to spot capital structure divergence.*

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import norm

def merton_implied_equity_vol(asset_value, debt_face, risk_free_rate, time_to_maturity, cds_spread):
    # Simplified Merton model relationship approximation
    d1 = (np.log(asset_value / debt_face) + (risk_free_rate + 0.5 * 0.2**2) * time_to_maturity) / (0.2 * np.sqrt(time_to_maturity))
```

- The midday session (11:30 AM - 2:00 PM EST) usually sees a distinct lull in volume and price action compared to the bookend hours.

```python
import pandas as pd
```

- False breakouts are highly prevalent during this window due to a lack of genuine institutional commitment behind price pushes.
- Volatility usually compresses until the European markets close and North American afternoon volume begins to ramp up.

### Summary
Pre-market trading signals capture how overnight news, macroeconomic data releases, and corporate earnings are processed before the regular market open. This after-hours and early-morning price discovery phase establishes initial sentiment, key resistance and support levels, and potential gap directions for the upcoming trading session.

### Key Points
- Price discovery occurs outside regular trading hours with significantly lower institutional liquidity and wider bid-ask spreads.
- Major earnings reports and unexpected macroeconomic or geopolitical news released overnight heavily dictate pre-market gap moves.
- Pre-market volume is a crucial filtering metric; low-volume moves often lack staying power and risk fading upon regular market open.
- Retail-dominated pre-market order flow can create exaggerated price spikes that do not reflect institutional intent.

### Code Example
*Calculate pre-market gap percentage and evaluate volume relative to historical averages*

```python
gap_pct = (pre_market_close - previous_close) / previous_close * 100
```

### Best Practices
- ✅ Wait until at least 8:30 AM EST to evaluate pre-market trends, as major economic data releases and deeper liquidity arrive then.

- ✅ Monitor pre-market relative volume compared to normal baseline levels to gauge genuine market conviction.

### Interview Questions
**Q:** Why might a stock gap up significantly in the pre-market on low volume and then reverse violently right after the market opens?  
**A:** Low-volume pre-market moves are driven by sparse liquidity, making the price susceptible to exaggerated reactions from small retail orders. When institutional liquidity enters at the open, the artificial price imbalance corrects, leading to a gap fill or reversal.  

**Q:** What key metrics should you examine alongside pre-market price change to determine if a gap is tradeable?  
**A:** Pre-market trading volume relative to average daily volume, the presence of fundamental catalysts (like earnings or FDA approvals), and alignment with pre-market index futures.
