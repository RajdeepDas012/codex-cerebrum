### Summary
Forward guidance represents a company's official forecast of future revenues, earnings, and operating margins. When management lowers this outlook, it acts as a powerful bearish catalyst that frequently triggers immediate institutional selling and multi-quarter multiple contraction.

### Key Points
- A guidance cut indicates that management has lost visibility or confidence in near-term demand, supply chain stability, or pricing power.
- Institutional investors and algorithms react violently to guidance reductions because valuation models rely heavily on future cash flow projections rather than historical results.
- Unlike a one-off earnings miss which might be blamed on temporary anomalies, a lowered outlook signals a structural or macroeconomic headwind.
- Stocks that reduce guidance often experience sustained downward momentum, meaning 'buying the dip' immediately can be a value trap.

### Code Example
*Scans for negative revisions in analyst forward estimates following an announcement*

```python
def evaluate_guidance_cut(current_price, previous_target, new_target):
    revision_pct = (new_target - previous_target) / previous_target
    if revision_pct < -0.05:
        return 'Bearish Signal: Exit long positions or initiate hedge'
    return 'Hold'
```

### Summary
A short squeeze occurs when a heavily shorted stock experiences rapid upward price movement, forcing short sellers to buy back shares to cover their positions, which further accelerates the price surge. Identifying the optimal setup conditions helps traders spot explosive asymmetric risk-reward opportunities before the momentum peaks.

### Key Points
- High short interest percentage of float (typically above 20% to 30%) creates a vulnerable underlying market structure.
- Low days to cover (short interest ratio) indicates that shorts cannot exit quickly without drastically moving the price up.
- High cost-to-borrow (CTB) and low share availability (hard-to-borrow status) increase the daily financial pressure on short sellers.
- A positive fundamental catalyst or sudden technical breakout acts as the immediate trigger forcing initial liquidations.

### Code Example
*Filters a dataset of stocks to identify prime candidates meeting classic short squeeze criteria.*

```python
import pandas as pd

def find_squeeze_candidates(df):
    return df[(df['short_float'] > 0.25) & (df['days_to_cover'] > 5.0) & (df['cost_to_borrow'] > 15.0)]
```

- ✅ Monitor real-time intraday volume spikes relative to average daily volume to confirm aggressive buyer momentum.
- ✅ Implement strict stop-loss rules because failed squeeze setups often retrace violently and quickly.

### Interview Questions

### Summary

### Key Points

- Gamma measures the rate of change in delta; high gamma near the strike price means delta changes rapidly as the stock price moves.
- When heavy volume flows into short-dated OTM calls, dealers buy stock as the price rises, pushing it higher and triggering even more hedging.
- The squeeze typically exhausts itself rapidly once options expire, volatility drops, or retail buying volume dries up.

- Rapid price movement forces rapid rehedging, creating an artificial supply or demand shock in the underlying stock.

### Code Example
*Simulates how aggregate dealer delta changes dynamically as the underlying stock price shifts.*

```python
import numpy as np

def calculate_dealer_hedge_adjustment(spot_prices, strikes, net_gamma):
    # Approximate change in delta given price shifts and net gamma profile
    delta_shifts = np.gradient(spot_prices) * net_gamma
    return np.cumsum(delta_shifts)
```

**Topic:** Vanna and charm effects — second order Greeks impact on price  
**Category:** `trading-strategies`  
**Difficulty:** `advanced`  
**Language:** `language-agnostic`  

### Summary
Vanna measures the sensitivity of option delta to changes in implied volatility, while charm measures the sensitivity of option delta to the passage of time (delta decay). Understanding these second-order Greeks allows advanced traders to anticipate subtle, non-linear shifts in market maker hedging flows before they become obvious in first-order Greeks.

### Key Points
- Vanna links spot price movements with changes in volatility, driving accelerated dealer re-hedging when IV spikes during market selloffs.
- Charm (or delta decay) dictates how option delta drifts systematically as expiration approaches, particularly forcing steady flows near OpEx.
- Positive vanna in out-of-the-money calls means rising volatility increases their delta, forcing dealers to buy underlying stock.
- Charm effects intensify drastically during the final week of options expiration, creating predictable intraday directional biases.

### Code Example
*Calculates approximate vanna and charm for a European option using Black-Scholes formulas.*

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.stats import norm

def calculate_vanna_charm(S, K, T, r, sigma):
    d1 = (np.log(S / K) + (r + 0.5 * sigma**2) * T) / (sigma * np.sqrt(T))
    d2 = d1 - sigma * np.sqrt(T)
    vanna = -np.exp(-d1**2 / 2) * d2 / (sqrt_2_pi := np.sqrt(2 * np.pi) * sigma * S) * S # simplified
    charm = -np.exp(-d1**2 / 2) * (2 * r * T - d2 * sigma * np.sqrt(T)) / (2 * T * sigma * np.sqrt(2 * np.pi))
    return vanna, charm
```

### Interview Questions
**Q:** How does vanna influence market maker hedging behavior during a sudden spike in implied volatility?  
**A:** As IV rises, positive vanna increases call delta (and negative vanna decreases put delta), forcing market makers who are short options to aggressively buy the underlying asset to maintain delta neutrality.  

**Q:** Why is charm particularly important to monitor in the final 48 hours of an option's life?  
**A:** Charm represents the time-derivative of delta; as time to maturity approaches zero, charm accelerates, causing rapid, automated shifts in dealer hedge ratios that can trigger intraday trend acceleration.

### Code Example
*Filters options trades to identify aggressive sweep orders hitting the ask price with volume exceeding open interest*
